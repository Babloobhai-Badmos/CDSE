Accessibility statement

A disability studies centre should be able to say how accessible its own website is — including where it falls short.

Our position

What we are aiming for

This site is built to meet WCAG 2.1 at level AA, and we are working towards the WCAG 2.2 AA criteria as well (principally the newer focus-visibility and contrast requirements). We treat that as a floor, not a badge: accessibility is part of the work the Centre exists to do, so a barrier here is a defect, not a nice-to-have.

We have not yet had an independent accessibility audit, and we have not yet tested every page with a screen reader. This page says so plainly rather than claiming otherwise.

What is in place

What we have done

  • Semantic HTML with one main landmark per page, a skip-to-content link, and labelled navigation.
  • Every control works from the keyboard, with a focus indicator that stays visible on both light and dark backgrounds.
  • On-page controls for text size (A−, A, A+) and a high-contrast theme, remembered between visits.
  • No autoplaying audio or video. Blog posts can be listened to, with a real player (play, pause, rewind, speed) or the browser's own read-aloud.
  • Text can be resized to 150% without the layout breaking, and the site is usable at 320px wide.
  • Touch targets are at least 44px on phones; the mobile menu closes on Escape and returns focus to the button that opened it.
  • Photographs carry alternative text; decorative marks are hidden from assistive technology.
  • Page structure uses real headings in order, so screen-reader users can navigate by heading.

Honestly

Known limitations

These are the gaps we know about. Some are ours to fix; some are outside our control. If you hit one, please tell us.

The blog list needs JavaScript

The blog index and post pages are rendered in the browser from a small data file. With JavaScript switched off, the post list is not shown — a plain message points to the archive on the older site instead. Everything else on the site works without JavaScript.

The forms are third-party

Membership, contact, accessibility reports and blog submissions use embedded Google Forms. We chose them because responses are stored and notified reliably, but we do not control their internal markup, labels or keyboard behaviour, and they may not meet the same standard as the rest of this site.

Some images load from the older site

The logo and photographs are currently served from nlujcdse.in rather than from this site. If that host is slow or unavailable, images may not appear. Moving them onto this domain is on our list.

Audio is synthetic

Audio versions of posts are generated with a text-to-speech voice. It can mispronounce legal terms, case names and Indian names. Where a post has no audio, the browser's own read-aloud is offered instead, and the full text is always on the page.

No formal audit yet

We have checked the site against the WCAG criteria we can test by hand, but have not run an independent audit or a full assistive-technology pass. We will publish the result here when we do.

English only

The site is in English. Hindi translation of the main pages is under consideration.

Tell us

If something here is a barrier

Please tell us what you were trying to do, what happened instead, and what would have helped. We welcome reports from anyone — you do not need to be a member of the University.

Report an accessibility issue Contact us

Or email cdse@nlujodhpur.ac.in. We aim to acknowledge accessibility reports within a week, and to fix or explain the problem within a month. If we cannot fix something quickly, we will say what we are doing instead and offer an alternative way to get the information.

Technical

How this site is built

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no framework or build step. It is tested in current versions of Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari on desktop and Android, and in Safari on iOS. It does not require a plugin, an account or a particular browser to read.
